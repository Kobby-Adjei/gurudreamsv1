# AirBridge: Target Segments, Market Size Signals, and Willingness to Pay ($15/mo)

Research date: 2026-11-23. Figures are labeled "reported" when they come from the company or from a major outlet citing the company, and "estimate" when they come from analysts or aggregators. Many 2026 figures reach us only through secondary sites. Those are flagged.

## Market size signals: AI coding agent and MCP adoption (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
The installed base of AI coding agents runs to tens of millions: Copilot is reported at about 50M users, Codex at 5–8M weekly, and Cursor at single-digit millions of MAU. Spending is in the billions: Claude Code is reported above $2.5B run-rate, and Cursor above $2B ARR. The relevant niche for AirBridge is much smaller: heavy agent users who run more than one machine and more than one vendor's agent. No source measures that niche directly.

Its run-rate was above $2.5B by Feb 2026, roughly double since Jan 1 (reported; Anthropic via Bloomberg) — [Bloomberg, 2026-02-20](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-20/the-surprise-hit-that-made-anthropic-into-an-ai-juggernaut-mlve4nc2); [Simon Willison, 2026-02-12](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Feb/12/anthropic/); [Constellation Research](https://www.constellationr.com/insights/news/anthropics-claude-code-revenue-doubled-jan-1)
- **Claude Code (later, unverified estimate):** about $8B run-rate around May 2026, attributed to Sacra and Anthropic disclosures. I could not confirm it in a primary Anthropic statement — [FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-claude-code-run-rate-agentic-layer/)
- **Anthropic overall:** $30B run-rate reported in April 2026, per [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/anthropic-says-it-hit-a-30-billion-revenue-run-rate-after-crazy-80x-growth/). ### Inferences
- A rough top-down count of people paying for Claude Code, Cursor, or Codex in late 2026 is low tens of millions. If 5–10% run agents across two or more personal machines, and 1–5% of those pay for a bridge, the reachable paid base is roughly 10k–100k users. At $15/mo that is about $2M–$18M ARR. This is an illustrative estimate, not a sourced number.
- Multi-vendor use is plausible because individual tools have large and overlapping penetration (for example, Cursor at 17.9% and Claude Code at 9.7% in the SO survey). No source quantifies the share of developers who use two or more agents.

### Gaps

- The Stack Overflow 2026 survey results were not yet found or retrieved.

## Multi-machine ownership among developers

### Takeaway
I found no rigorous survey that measures how many developers own or actively use two or more computers. The evidence is indirect: large homelab and local-LLM communities, and heavy cross-platform OS use. The best direct evidence of demand is that Anthropic built cross-machine session messaging into Claude Code.

### Cited Findings
- No developer survey reporting device counts surfaced. The available evidence is limited to informal dev.to polls and threads, an ad hoc blog poll (42% desktop with multiple monitors vs 46% laptop only), and old general-population studies — [dev.to "How many computers do you use"](https://dev.to/ben/how-many-computers-do-you-use-4mp9); [engineered.at](https://engineered.at/articles/computing-survey-results)
- **OS mix:**
  - Stack Overflow 2025 kept its OS questions. The official professional-use split was not retrieved.
  - A third-party table shows professional use at Windows about 47.6–58.7%, macOS about 31.8–33.2%, and Ubuntu about 26.9%. Its years are ambiguous and the tables conflict, so treat it as unreliable — [commandlinux](https://commandlinux.com/statistics/developer-os-preference-stack-overflow-survey/)
  - SO 2025 notes that Android (29%) overtook Ubuntu as a personal OS — [Stack Overflow blog](https://stackoverflow.blog/2025/07/29/developers-remain-willing-but-reluctant-to-use-ai-the-2025-developer-survey-results-are-here/)
- **Community sizes:**
  - r/homelab has about 1.1M members (March 2026) — [Hive Index](https://thehiveindex.com/communities/r-homelab)
  - r/LocalLLaMA has about 686k–762k members (May 2026 snapshots) — [AgentsIndex](https://agentsindex.ai/r-localllama); [GummySearch](https://gummysearch.com/tools/subreddit-finder/ai-developers/)
- Users run Claude Code on a desktop Mac plus a headless Mac mini and switch between Remote Control sessions — [MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/stories/hands-on-with-claude-code-remote-control/)
- Guides exist for SSH-tunnelling to Claude Code on other machines, which shows a DIY workaround is in use — [Compiled Thoughts](https://compiledthoughts.pages.dev/blog/claude-code-remote-ssh-tunnel)

### Inferences
- Homelab and local-LLM communities (about 1.8M combined, with overlap) are the clearest pool of people who own a GPU box or server alongside a laptop. They are also the most DIY-inclined and the most likely to use free Tailscale or SSH instead of paying.
- A significant minority of professional developers likely use a different OS for work and personal use, and likely run WSL. That implies heterogeneous machines but does not prove multi-machine agent workflows.

### Gaps
- There is no quantitative source on the share of developers with two or more personal machines, or with a laptop plus a GPU workstation.
- The official SO 2025 OS percentages were not extracted. The page was truncated, and the CSV would be needed.

## Candidate segments: who has the pain and the budget

### Takeaway
The strongest early segment is the individual power user: an indie or solo developer, or an AI/ML engineer, who already pays $100–$200/mo for Claude Max, ChatGPT Pro, or Cursor Ultra and runs agents on several machines. Students and international developers are price-sensitive. Enterprise developers are constrained by IT policy, and Anthropic's own controls let admins switch off cross-machine features. The largest risk is that Claude Code now offers cross-machine messaging natively at no extra cost.

### Cited Findings
- **Competing native feature (critical):** Claude Code's cross-session messaging lets Claude "list and message your other Claude Code sessions on this machine, and reach your sessions on other machines or in the cloud."
  - Cross-machine messages travel "through Anthropic servers, arriving over that machine's Remote Control connection."
  - It requires v2.1.225+ and a claude.ai sign-in. It is not available with API keys or on Bedrock, Vertex, or Foundry.
  - It sends plain text only and is on by default.

  Source: [Claude Code docs – Cross-session messaging](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/cross-session-messaging)
- **Remote Control:**
  - Available on Pro, Max, Ultra, Team, and Enterprise plans.
  - On Team and Enterprise an Owner must enable it.
  - Server mode runs up to 32 concurrent sessions.
  - "Session transcript ... is stored on Anthropic servers."

  Source: [Claude Code docs – Remote Control](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/remote-control)
- **Enterprise controls:** admins can turn messaging off org-wide with managed settings, denying `SendMessage` and `ListAgents` and setting `crossSessionInbound: refuse`. `isolatePeerMachines` requires approval for cross-machine messages. This shows enterprises want control over cross-machine agent traffic — [Cross-session messaging docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/cross-session-messaging)
- **Heavy users already spend $100–$200/mo on AI coding:**
  - Claude Max costs $100 (5x) or $200 (20x) — [TechCrunch, 2025-04-09](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/09/anthropic-rolls-out-a-200-per-month-claude-subscription)
  - OpenAI added a $100 ChatGPT Pro tier on April 9, 2026, with 5x Plus Codex usage, aimed at Claude Max — [TNW, 2026-04-12](https://thenextweb.com/news/openais-new-100-chatgpt-pro-plan-targets-claude-max-with-five-times-the-codex-access)
  - Indie-hacker comparisons treat $100–$200/mo stacks as normal — [devtoolpicks](https://devtoolpicks.com/blog/chatgpt-pro-100-vs-claude-max-vs-cursor-indie-hackers-2026)
- **Codex usage is broadening** to knowledge workers, and India grew 27x in 2026, which signals a large international and price-sensitive base — [Axios](https://axios.com/2026/06/02/openai-codex-knowledge-workers); [IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.co.in/codex-weekly-active-users-india-surge-27x-so-far-2026-openai-902440)
- **AI/ML engineers and local-model users:** r/LocalLLaMA has about 700k members — [AgentsIndex](https://agentsindex.ai/r-localllama)

### Inferences
- **Indie/solo and freelance power users:** high pain (agents on a laptop and a desktop or Mac mini) and proven spend (already $100+/mo on models). A $15 add-on is about 7–15% of their existing AI bill. They are the best initial segment, but only if AirBridge does something Claude's native feature does not.
- **AI/ML engineers with GPU workstations:** clear pain (a laptop driving a GPU box). They often have employer budgets but are DIY-capable with SSH and Tailscale. This is a good segment if the product handles cross-vendor work (for example, Cursor on a laptop handing to Codex or Claude on a GPU box).
- **Multi-vendor users:** this is the main defensible wedge. Anthropic's feature covers only Claude-to-Claude, and its cross-machine messages route through Anthropic servers. A vendor-neutral, private (peer-to-peer or end-to-end encrypted) bridge across Claude, Cursor, and Codex is the differentiated position.
- **Small agencies and teams:** possible later with a team tier, but the product brief is for linking "a user's own computers," not team sharing.
- **Students:** high usage and low willingness to pay. Suitable for free-tier or education pricing, not as a revenue base.
- **Enterprise developers:** likely blocked. Unapproved network tunnels and agent-to-agent channels conflict with IT policy, and vendors already ship admin kill-switches. This segment needs SSO, audit, and self-hosting, which is not a $15 product.

### Gaps
- I found no data on how many Claude Code users actively use Remote Control or cross-session messaging.
- I did not verify whether Cursor or Codex have shipped equivalent cross-machine handoff features. The other researchers' competitor notes should cover this.
- There is no survey of how many developers use two or more AI coding agents at once.

## Willingness to pay: comparable tool pricing and conversion benchmarks

### Takeaway
$15/mo sits at the high end for a single-purpose utility. Developer utilities cluster at $5–$10/mo per user (Raycast Pro $8–10, Tailscale Standard $8). Connectivity in particular is anchored at $0 for personal use, because Tailscale's free Personal plan now covers six users and unlimited devices. Freemium dev tools typically convert about 2–5% of free users to paid.

### Cited Findings
- **Tailscale:**
  - Retired Personal Plus in April 2026. The free Personal plan now supports up to six users.
  - Standard is $8 per user per month, and Premium is $18 per user per month.
  - User devices are unlimited on every plan.

  Sources: [Tailscale blog – pricing v4, 2026-04-08](https://tailscale.com/blog/pricing-v4); [Tailscale pricing](https://tailscale.com/pricing/); [SSD Nodes guide](https://www.ssdnodes.com/learn/tailscale-pricing-explained)
- **Raycast:** Pro is about $8/mo annual or $10/mo monthly. Pro with Advanced AI is $16–$20 (sources conflict). There is a new $50/mo Max plan with usage-based AI — [Raycast Pro](https://raycast.com/pro); [Raycast blog](https://www.raycast.com/blog/changing-how-raycast-ai-is-priced); [PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/raycast)
- **Warp:** has a $50/mo business tier with SSO and SOC 2 (Nov 2025). Warp moved to usage and credit-based AI pricing — [Tessl](https://tessl.io/blog/warp-joins-the-pricing-pivot-sweeping-ai-developer-tools)
- **Claude:** Max is $100 or $200/mo — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/09/anthropic-rolls-out-a-200-per-month-claude-subscription)
- **ChatGPT Pro:** tiers at $100 and $200, and reportedly $500 by Nov 2026 per a secondary source — [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/openais-new-100-chatgpt-pro-plan-targets-claude-max-with-five-times-the-codex-access); [tech-insider](https://tech-insider.org/chatgpt-vs-claude-vs-gemini-vs-grok-subscription-pricing-2026/)
- **Cursor:**
  - Pro was historically $20/mo, and Ultra is $200/mo (about $400 of API usage).
  - One late-2026 secondary source lists a tier set of Free, Plus $8, Premium $30, Super $100, and Ultra $200. That is unverified and conflicts with other sources.

  Sources: [Cursor forum](https://forum.cursor.com/t/cursor-200-vs-claude-max-cursor-usage-limits-and-trade-offs/148298/4); [devtoolpicks](https://dev.to/devtoolpicks/chatgpt-pro-100-vs-claude-max-vs-cursor-which-ai-coding-subscription-is-worth-it-for-indie-1di2); [tech-insider](https://tech-insider.org/chatgpt-vs-claude-vs-gemini-vs-grok-subscription-pricing-2026/)
- **Conversion benchmarks:**
  - OpenView's 2022 benchmarks (via BoldStart) show developer tools with a median 10% visitor-to-signup rate, and a median free-to-paid conversion of about 5%, roughly half that of non-developer products. Freemium beats free trial on signup rate (9% vs 5%) — [BoldStart Dev Toolkit](https://boldstart.vc/devtoolkit/an-alternative-to-nps-for-dev-tools/); [OpenView 2022 benchmarks](https://openviewpartners.com/2022-product-benchmarks/)
  - OpenView 2020 found a freemium median free-to-paid of about 7%, against about 14% for free trials — [OpenView freemium vs free trial](https://openviewpartners.com/blog/freemium-vs-free-trial)
  - ChartMogul's conversion report is a further benchmark source — [ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/)
  - Commentary puts typical SaaS freemium at 2–5%, with developer tools lower. That source is lower quality — [Monetizely](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/whats-the-right-ratio-of-free-to-paid-users-in-developer-saas)
- **Pricing trend:** AI developer tools have pivoted from flat subscriptions to usage and credit-based pricing (Warp, Raycast, Cursor) — [Tessl](https://tessl.io/blog/warp-joins-the-pricing-pivot-sweeping-ai-developer-tools); [Raycast blog](https://www.raycast.com/blog/changing-how-raycast-ai-is-priced)

### Inferences
- $15/mo is plausible only as a "pro power-user" tier. It is about twice the going rate for single-purpose dev utilities ($8–10). Free Tailscale plus free native Claude cross-machine messaging set a strong $0 anchor for the "connect my machines" part. The price must be justified by cross-vendor orchestration, privacy, and reliability, not connectivity.
- **Suggested model:**
  - Freemium free tier: two machines and Claude only, or a limited number of handoffs per day.
  - Pro at about $8–10/mo, or about $79–96/yr: unlimited machines and cross-vendor handoff.
  - An optional higher tier at about $15–20 for teams, audit logs, or self-hosted relay.
  - A one-time or lifetime license is attractive to the homelab crowd but conflicts with ongoing relay costs.
  - Regional and student discounts would help, given Codex's growth in India.
- **Illustrative conversion math (not sourced):** at 2–5% free-to-paid, reaching 1,000 paying users at $15 (about $180k ARR) needs roughly 20k–50k active free users.

### Gaps
- Termius, ngrok, Zed, and Setapp prices were not retrieved in this pass. Those searches returned nothing usable.
- I found no data on pricing of agent-orchestration tools such as Conductor, Claude Squad, or Vibe Kanban. Many are free or open source.
- I found no direct survey of student or international developers' price sensitivity for dev tools.
- I found no evidence of developers paying specifically for agent-to-agent connectivity utilities. The category is too new.
